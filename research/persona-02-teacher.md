# Persona interview 02 — Daniel, 41, secondary school teacher (14 yrs)

Context: Microsoft school (Outlook, Teams, SIMS). Also runs D of E and junior
football. Academic calendar published as PDF each August, then amended ~20x/year
by email. School laptop is locked down.

## 1. The incident
Y11 fieldwork trip. Payment deadline moved forward a week by the finance office in
a one-line email **buried under a paragraph about the photocopier**. Read it at
10:40pm, meant to diarise it, didn't. Eleven kids unpaid at cutoff.
**Two kids didn't go on the trip.** That's the cost — not his time.

## 2. The August PDF (the flagship artifact)
Landscape A4, 2 pages, made in Word, exported to PDF. Rows = weeks, columns =
Mon-Fri. Term dates bold at top ("Autumn Term 1: Mon 2 Sept - Fri 25 Oct"). INSET
days shaded grey, "STAFF ONLY" in 8pt. Half terms = merged cell across a week.
Exam windows in a separate block as **"Y11 Mocks: w/c 18 Nov"** — a week
commencing, not a date; the real timetable is a different doc from the exams
officer in October.

### THE AMENDMENT PROBLEM (he says this is the whole problem)
The PDF is wrong by October. INSET moves for a strike day or a boiler. Parents'
evening shifts a week, arriving as: *"Please note the Y9 parents' evening has
moved from 12th to 19th March."*

> "A tool that only does one-time imports gives me two parents' evenings in my
> calendar and no idea which is live. That's worse than nothing. I need to
> photograph that email and have it FIND THE EXISTING EVENT AND MOVE IT."

## 3. Recurrence
**Two-week timetable (Week A / Week B)**, 5 periods a day. Teaches 9C Tuesday
period 3 Week A only — "weekly" guessing is catastrophic. A/B doesn't alternate
cleanly: an INSET Monday means the following week is still Week A. Cover lessons
assigned 8:15am same day. Football Thursdays, stops mid-May for exams, restarts
September. D of E = three unrelated Saturdays.

Cost of error: turns up to a room with 30 kids who aren't his — or 30 kids sit
unsupervised. **That's a safeguarding conversation.**

> "Don't guess my timetable. My timetable is in SIMS and it's correct. Do the
> one-off stuff."

## 4. Deadlines vs events
Hard: data drops (3/yr, SLT chase), report deadlines, trip payment cutoffs, exam
entry deadlines. These are "by end of Friday", not "at 2pm". Needs them visually
distinct AND **a warning a week out** — a 15-min reminder on the day is already
too late. "That's not noise, that's the half I actually care about."

## 5. Sharing — hard institutional walls
- **Parents:** NOT ALLOWED to send a link from a personal tool. Must go via the
  school system so it's logged. What works: plain email with dates as **text**.
- **Department (5 people, all Outlook):** Outlook invite or `.ics` attachment.
  Not a link to your website.
- **Football squad:** parents' WhatsApp group. **Pasteable text**, nothing else.
- **Students:** never. No pupil-facing link from an unapproved tool.

Currently handles changes by sending a second WhatsApp saying "IGNORE THE LAST ONE."

> "If your tool can update a shared set and tell people what changed, that's
> genuinely the feature. But it has to reach them where they are."

## 6. After the import (40 events, 7 wrong)
Checks all 40 -> tool saved him nothing. To earn trust: **per-event confidence**,
low-confidence ones in a review pile at the top, and **show the crop of the PDF
each date came from, side by side**. Then he checks 7, not 40. Multi-select bulk
edit with "shift these all by one week" is essential.

## 7. Dealbreakers
- Cannot install anything on the school laptop. Browser or personal phone only.
- Pupil names on a rota -> personal device -> third-party server = DPO problem.
- Work calendar is **Outlook, not Google**. Google-only sync makes it a hobby app.
- Won't pay ~GBP 6/mo personally for something the school should provide.

## 8. Verdict
Would use it for the August PDF + amendment emails, on his phone, **for his own
calendar only**, if it did Outlook and cost him nothing. **Dies at sharing** — the
moment you touch parents or pupils you're in safeguarding/IT territory he cannot
authorise.

> "The real product is the one the school buys... Sell it to my business manager,
> not to me."
