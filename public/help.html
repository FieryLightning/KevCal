How to use it

KevCal

Point your phone at anything with a date on it — a school letter, a poster, an appointment card — and it becomes a calendar entry you check before you keep it.

1 Put it on your phone

  1. Open KevCal in Safari on your phone, using the address the app printed when it started.
  2. Tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
  3. Open it from the icon. It runs full screen, like any other app.
The laptop has to be awake. KevCal lives on the computer, not the phone — the phone is just looking at it. If the laptop is asleep or on a different network, the page will not load.

2 Capture something

Tap the camera button in the middle of the bar at the bottom. You get four ways in:

  • Take a photo — a letter in your hand, a poster on a wall, a noticeboard.
  • From your photos — a screenshot you already took.
  • A file or PDF — a newsletter or a term calendar.
  • Paste some text — an email or a message.

Straight-on beats artistic. It reads fine print, so don't crop it out.

3 Read what came back

Nothing is ever guessed. Where KevCal could not be certain, it asks instead of inventing an answer.

The date stamp is the day. Red means a deadline — something to act before, like a payment or a form. Blue is an event, something to turn up to.

Needs your answer Something could not be worked out — a letter that says “after half term” without saying when that is. These are red, and they block: you cannot add the batch until you answer.
Worth a check A choice was made and is being declared — a year nobody printed, or “3.30” with no am or pm. These are amber, and they do not block. The alternative is one tap away.

Anything with no flag was read cleanly. Quiet means confident.

4 Fix anything that is wrong

  • One tap — where a flag offers buttons (3:30pm / No, 3:30am), tapping one settles it.
  • Tap the entry itself to open it and edit the title, date, time, place or notes.
  • Check where it came from — the editor shows the exact patch of the page it was read from. Tap that to see the whole document with the spot boxed.
  • Remove takes a single entry out; Discard throws away the whole import.

5 Into your real calendar

Do this once, in Settings → Your calendar → Subscribe on this device, and you never have to do it again.

Your phone's calendar subscribes to KevCal and reads it live. Everything you keep turns up there on its own. Correct a date here and it moves there. Undo an import and those dates leave again.

Why not just export a file? A calendar file is a copy. Once it lands in Apple Calendar it is on its own — undo an import afterwards and the dates stay there forever, and you are back to deleting them one at a time. Subscribing is how “undo” keeps meaning something.

Two things to know. Subscribed entries are read-only on the phone, so make corrections in KevCal, not in Calendar. And the phone refreshes on its own schedule — usually within the hour, not instantly.

Reminders are written inside each entry, so they go off whether or not KevCal is running. Deadlines get several, counting down; events get one the day before.

The one-off Add to Calendar button still exists on the screen after you add dates, for when you want a copy that stands alone — handing a trip's dates to somebody else, say.

6 Undo, always

Every capture is one import. Go to Imports and Undo takes the whole thing back out in one tap; Restore puts it back. Nothing you do here is permanent except Delete, which also deletes the original photo.

Import an updated letter and KevCal compares it with the old one — “1 changed, 1 added” — instead of giving you two of everything. Anything you edited by hand is left alone.

7 Sending dates to someone else

From any import, Share. Whoever you send it to installs nothing and signs up for nothing: plain text to paste into a message, a calendar file, or a link they can subscribe to. You can stop a shared link working at any time from the Shared tab.

8 When something looks wrong

What you seeWhat to do
It found nothing Usually a photo too dark, too angled, or cropped. Retake it straight on. For a screenshot of text, paste the text instead — that always works.
A date is plainly wrong Tap it and correct it. Then tap the source crop — if the page really does say that, the document is wrong, not KevCal.
“That’s already gone” The date is in the past. Often the year was not printed and the wrong one was assumed — the flag offers the other year.
“Reading is paused” The month’s spending cap is reached. Imports still work, read on the laptop instead, just less well.
The page will not load on your phone The laptop is asleep, or you are on a different network from it.

9 What it will never do

  • Invent a repeat. “Every other Tuesday” is offered as a button you press, never applied for you — and a repeat always needs an end date.
  • Round an impossible date. “29 February 2027” is reported as unreadable, not quietly moved to the 28th.
  • Hide what it assumed. If it had to choose, it says so on the card, in words.

Where your things go. Everything lives on the computer running KevCal — a database and the original images. No account, nothing synced anywhere. The one exception: the page you import is uploaded to whichever reader is switched on, so it can be read. The line at the bottom of every screen always names it.
